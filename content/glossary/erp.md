Before an ERP, most companies run on a mix of spreadsheets, accounting software and departmental tools. Each holds part of the picture, and staff spend time re-typing and reconciling data between them.

An ERP replaces that with **modules that share one database**. When sales confirms an order, the warehouse sees it, stock is reserved and finance can invoice it, without anyone copying data.

## What does an ERP usually include?

- Sales and customer management
- Purchasing and supplier management
- Inventory and warehouse
- Manufacturing or project costing (where relevant)
- Accounting, tax and financial reporting

## Which ERP is right for a mid-sized Indonesian company?

There is no single answer. Options range from local accounting-led systems to international platforms. The right choice depends on your processes, transaction volumes, integration needs, budget and the availability of local support. The implementation (process mapping, data migration and training) usually matters more than the brand.
